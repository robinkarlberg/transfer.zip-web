<TLDR>
There is **no AirDrop for Windows**: Apple ships it only on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

The closest cross-platform match is **Transfer.zip Quick Transfer**: browser-based, end-to-end encrypted, no app to install, and unlike AirDrop it works between devices that aren't even in the same building.
</TLDR>

## Why you can't just install AirDrop

AirDrop is genuinely great inside Apple's fence: direct device-to-device Wi-Fi, no size limit that Apple documents, zero setup. But it only exists on Apple hardware. The moment one end of the transfer is a Windows PC or an Android phone, AirDrop is off the table, and Apple has never shipped a Windows client.

It's also strictly a proximity tool: both devices within roughly 10 meters with Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on. "AirDrop this to my colleague across town" was never a thing.

What you want from an alternative depends on which of those two walls you hit: the platform wall or the distance wall. The options below cover both.

## 1. Transfer.zip - AirDrop convenience on every platform, at any distance

![A screenshot of Transfer.zip homepage.](/img/content/services/transfer-zip.png)

**Quick Transfer** runs in the browser, which is the one platform every device already shares. Pick files on one device; on the other, scan the QR code or type the 6-digit code at transfer.zip/quick. The files stream directly between the two devices, **end-to-end encrypted**, with **no size limit**.

iPhone to Windows, Android to Mac, PC to PC across the planet: the pairing works the same, and the devices don't need to share a network, or a room.

## 2. Quick Share - Google's AirDrop, for Android and Windows

Google's Quick Share is the closest literal equivalent: nearby transfers, built into Android, with an official Windows app (64-bit Windows 10 and up, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on, devices within about 5 meters).

Two fences to know about: there's no Quick Share for Mac, and the Windows app doesn't talk to iPhones. Interestingly, the fences between Quick Share and AirDrop themselves have started coming down: since late 2025, recent Pixel and flagship Android phones can exchange files with AirDrop devices directly. That interop is phone-to-phone, though, and doesn't help the Windows case.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Built into Android, official Windows app</Pro>
<Pro>Fast nearby transfers, AirDrop-style</Pro>
<Pro>Free</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>No macOS app, no iPhone support on Windows</Con>
<Con>Proximity and Bluetooth required</Con>
<Con>Works best signed into a Google account</Con>
</BadFor>

## 3. PairDrop - Snapdrop's spirit, in the browser

PairDrop recreates the AirDrop radar in a browser tab: devices on the same Wi-Fi discover each other automatically and transfer directly over WebRTC. It's the open-source continuation of the original Snapdrop, and it adds cross-network transfers if you pair devices with a code first.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Browser-based, nothing to install</Pro>
<Pro>Automatic discovery on the same network</Pro>
<Pro>Free and open source</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>Same-network by default; pairing needed for more</Con>
<Con>Community project without commercial support</Con>
</BadFor>

<ExternalLink href="https://pairdrop.net">Try PairDrop</ExternalLink>

## 4. LocalSend - The offline option

LocalSend is an open-source app for all major platforms that transfers files directly over the local network, no internet required at all. It's what to reach for on a plane, in a dead zone, or on a network you don't trust. Every participating device needs the app installed, which makes it better for your own device fleet than for handing a file to a stranger.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Works completely offline</Pro>
<Pro>All major platforms, including Mac and iPhone</Pro>
<Pro>Free, open source, no ads</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>App required on both devices</Con>
<Con>Same local network only</Con>
</BadFor>

<ExternalLink href="https://localsend.org">Try LocalSend</ExternalLink>

### Choosing the Right AirDrop Alternative

**If you need:**

1. **iPhone to Windows PC right now** → Transfer.zip in the browser; there is no native tool for this pair
2. **Android to Windows regularly** → Quick Share, it's built for exactly that
3. **The AirDrop radar feeling on mixed devices** → PairDrop on a shared Wi-Fi
4. **Transfers with no internet at all** → LocalSend
5. **Sending to someone in another city** → Transfer.zip; every nearby-sharing tool is out by definition

**Why Transfer.zip stands out:**
- One method that covers every platform pair, instead of a different app per combination
- Distance doesn't matter: same room or another country, the code works the same
- End-to-end encrypted with no size cap, so it also replaces AirDrop for the 20 GB video file
