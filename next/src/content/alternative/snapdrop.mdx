<TLDR>
Snapdrop was quietly **acquired by LimeWire**. Files that used to travel directly between your devices now go through **LimeWire's servers**.

The top alternative is **Transfer.zip**: browser-to-browser, end-to-end encrypted, no size limit, and it works even when the two devices are on **different networks**.
</TLDR>

## What happened to Snapdrop?

Snapdrop was the go-to "AirDrop in the browser": open two tabs on the same Wi-Fi, and files moved directly between devices over WebRTC. Then, in early 2025, users noticed the site behaving differently, and LimeWire later confirmed it had acquired the project.

The technical change is the part that matters: instead of a direct device-to-device connection, snapdrop.net now uploads your file to LimeWire's servers and hands you a sharing link. What made Snapdrop special is exactly the thing that got removed. The original open-source repository remains public but is effectively frozen under the new ownership.

If you came to Snapdrop for direct, private transfers, here's where to go now.

## 1. Transfer.zip - Direct transfers that also work across networks

![A screenshot of Transfer.zip homepage.](/img/content/services/transfer-zip.png)

Transfer.zip's **Quick Transfer** covers the Snapdrop use case and removes its biggest constraint: the two devices don't need to share a Wi-Fi network. Pick your files in the browser, then either scan the QR code, type the 6-digit code on the other device, or send the link. Files stream directly between the browsers, **end-to-end encrypted**, with **no size limit** and nothing stored on any server.

Like the original Snapdrop, it's [open source](https://github.com/robinkarlberg/transfer.zip-web), so the "your files don't touch our servers" claim is checkable rather than a promise.

<Pro>Works across different networks, not just same Wi-Fi</Pro>
<Pro>End-to-end encrypted by default</Pro>
<Pro>No file size limit</Pro>
<Pro>QR code and 6-digit code pairing</Pro>
<Pro>Open source</Pro>
<Pro>No app or account needed</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>Both devices must be online during the transfer</Con>
<Con>Stored transfers (for offline recipients) require a paid plan</Con>
</BadFor>

## 2. PairDrop - The community fork of the original Snapdrop

PairDrop is what Snapdrop's code became in better hands: a GPL-licensed fork that kept the direct WebRTC transfers and the familiar radar interface. Devices on the same network see each other automatically, and unlike the original, you can also **pair devices across networks** with a 6-digit code or join a temporary public room.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Free, open source, actively maintained</Pro>
<Pro>Same-network discovery like classic Snapdrop</Pro>
<Pro>Cross-network transfers via device pairing</Pro>
<Pro>Self-hostable with Docker</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>Cross-network use needs a pairing step first</Con>
<Con>No stored mode for offline recipients</Con>
<Con>Community project, no commercial support</Con>
</BadFor>

<ExternalLink href="https://pairdrop.net">Try PairDrop</ExternalLink>

## 3. LocalSend - An app instead of a browser tab

LocalSend is an open-source app for Windows, Mac, Linux, Android, and iOS that transfers files directly between devices on the same local network, with no internet required at all. The catch is the reason it's third on this list: everyone involved has to install the app first, which is exactly the step Snapdrop existed to avoid.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Free and open source</Pro>
<Pro>Works fully offline on a local network</Pro>
<Pro>Apps for every major platform</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>Requires installing an app on both devices</Con>
<Con>Same-network only</Con>
<Con>No browser-only option for the other side</Con>
</BadFor>

<ExternalLink href="https://localsend.org">Try LocalSend</ExternalLink>

## 4. Quick Share - Built in, if you're on Android and Windows

Google's Quick Share is preinstalled on Android and available as a Windows app (64-bit Windows 10 and up, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on, devices within a few meters). Between those two platforms it's a solid Snapdrop replacement with nothing to install on the phone side.

The ecosystem walls are the problem: there's no Quick Share for Mac, and it doesn't talk to iPhones.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Preinstalled on Android</Pro>
<Pro>Official Windows app</Pro>
<Pro>Fast nearby transfers</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>No Mac or iPhone support</Con>
<Con>Requires Bluetooth and physical proximity</Con>
<Con>Windows app setup nudges you toward a Google account</Con>
</BadFor>

<ExternalLink href="https://www.android.com/better-together/quick-share-app/">Get Quick Share for Windows</ExternalLink>

## 5. AirDrop - If everyone's on Apple

For iPhone-to-Mac and Mac-to-Mac, AirDrop is still the smoothest nearby option: no size limit that Apple documents, direct peer-to-peer connection, already installed. It remains Apple-only, needs Bluetooth and Wi-Fi enabled, and both devices within roughly 10 meters.

<GoodFor label="Pros:">
<Pro>Built into every iPhone, iPad, and Mac</Pro>
<Pro>Direct device-to-device, no server</Pro>
<Pro>No documented size limit</Pro>
</GoodFor>
<BadFor label="Cons:">
<Con>Apple devices only</Con>
<Con>Proximity required</Con>
<Con>No Windows, Android, or Linux</Con>
</BadFor>

### Choosing the Right Snapdrop Alternative

**If you need:**

1. **The same "open a tab and send" experience** → Transfer.zip or PairDrop, both browser-based with nothing to install
2. **Transfers between different networks** → Transfer.zip works that way by default; PairDrop needs a pairing step
3. **Fully offline local transfers** → LocalSend, if installing an app on every device is acceptable
4. **Single-ecosystem sharing** → Quick Share (Android/Windows) or AirDrop (Apple)

**Why Transfer.zip stands out:**
- The devices never need to be near each other or on the same Wi-Fi
- End-to-end encryption by default, with open-source code to prove it
- No size limit, so it replaces Snapdrop for the big files too
- QR and 6-digit code pairing feel instantly familiar to Snapdrop users
