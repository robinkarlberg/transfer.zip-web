The pattern: services that *store* your file have to pay for that storage, so free tiers stay small. The way past the wall is to either stream the file instead of storing it, or find the rare generous free host.

## Method 4: Split the File (Last Resort)

Stuck with a service capped below your file size? [7-Zip](https://www.7-zip.org/) can slice any file into chunks: right-click → **Add to archive** → **Split to volumes** → e.g. `2900M` to fit a 3 GB cap. The recipient downloads all parts and extracts the `.001` file.

It works, but it multiplies the number of things that can go wrong (one missing part breaks the archive), so use it only when the service is non-negotiable.

## FAQ

**How long does sending 10 GB take?**
At 100 Mbps it's roughly 15 minutes; at 20 Mbps, about an hour. With Quick Transfer the sender and receiver run simultaneously, so that's the total time, not per side. The [download time calculator](/tools/download-time-calculator) gives you exact numbers.

**Can I send 100 GB or more for free?**
Yes. Quick Transfer has no cap, since the file streams between devices instead of being stored. Plug both machines in, prefer a wired connection for speed, and keep the tabs open.

**Can I email a 10 GB file?**
No. Email tops out at 20-25 MB. Send a link instead; the recipient downloads in the browser.

**What if the transfer gets interrupted?**
A Quick Transfer stops if either side goes offline, and nothing partial is left on any server. Just start it again. For flaky connections, a stored transfer (Method 2) is more forgiving, since the recipient can retry the download.

**Is the free unlimited transfer really free?**
Yes. Quick Transfer costs nothing, needs no account, and has no size limit. The paid plans exist for stored transfers, when you need links that outlive your browser tab.
