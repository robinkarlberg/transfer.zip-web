## The handoff is part of the deliverable

You spent weeks on the project. Then the delivery is a WeTransfer link with an ad for someone else's product next to your client's files, or a Google Drive link that throws a permission error because their work account isn't whitelisted.

A good client file workflow needs four things:

1. **No friction for the client.** No account creation, no permission requests, no app installs.
2. **Your brand, not the tool's.** The download page is a client touchpoint.
3. **Proof of delivery.** You should know whether the files were downloaded without asking.
4. **A way to receive files too.** Collecting source material is half the battle.

Here's how to cover all four.

## Step 2: Track Downloads Instead of Asking

Every Transfer.zip transfer shows **views and downloads** in your dashboard. That replaces an entire genre of awkward email:

- The invoice-adjacent "just confirming you received the files" nudge.
- Wondering whether the deadline slipped because the client never opened the delivery.
- Re-sending files that were downloaded fine the first time.

If the link was viewed but not downloaded, you know exactly what your follow-up should say.

## Step 3: Collect Files from Clients with a Request Link

Getting files *from* clients is usually worse than sending them: material arrives across four email threads, two chat apps, and a USB stick at the kickoff meeting.

A Transfer.zip **file request** turns that into one link:

1. In the dashboard, create a request with a name and a short description of what you need.
2. Send the link, or have Transfer.zip email it to the client directly.
3. The client uploads in the browser. No account needed on their side.
4. Everything lands in your **Received** tab, grouped under the request.

The upload page carries your branding too, so even the "please send us your assets" step looks like your process instead of a scramble. More detail in [how to request files from someone](/how-to/request-files).

## FAQ

**Do my clients need a Transfer.zip account?**
No. Downloading needs nothing but the link, and uploading to a file request works straight from the browser.

**How large can client deliveries be?**
Up to 1 TB per stored transfer on the Pro plan. For anything beyond that, Quick Transfer has no size limit at all, as long as both sides stay online during the transfer.

**Can I use my own domain for download links?**
Yes, on the Pro and Teams plans. Connect a subdomain like `files.yourcompany.com` and links carry your name end to end.

**What happens when a link expires?**
The files become unavailable and are cleaned up. You choose the expiry when creating the transfer, up to 365 days on Pro, and you can delete a transfer early at any time.

**Is it secure enough for client work under NDA?**
Stored transfers are encrypted, and Quick Transfers are end-to-end encrypted, meaning not even Transfer.zip can read them. For the strictest material, see [how to share files securely](/how-to/share-files/securely).
