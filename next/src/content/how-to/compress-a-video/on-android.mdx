<TLDR>
On a **Samsung**, the Gallery app resizes videos with no extra app. On other Androids, **CapCut** exports at lower resolution and bitrate for free.

Compressing to *share* is often the wrong move, though: a **Transfer.zip** link sends the original at any size, straight from the phone's browser.
</TLDR>

## First, why are phone videos so big?

Modern phones shoot 4K by default, and 4K runs to hundreds of megabytes per minute. Compression means re-encoding at a lower resolution or bitrate: 4K down to 1080p or 720p cuts the size by 70-90%, and for watching on another phone, the difference is barely visible.

One thing that doesn't work: zipping. Files by Google can zip videos, but video is already compressed, so the zip comes out nearly the same size. Actual shrinking requires re-encoding.

---

## Method 1: Samsung Gallery (No App Needed, Samsung Only)

Galaxy phones that shoot 4K can resize video right in the Gallery app:

1. Open the video in **Gallery** and tap **Edit** (the pencil).
2. Tap the **three-dot menu** → **Size and format**.
3. Pick a lower resolution (FHD or HD) and save.

Samsung notes that big videos take a few minutes to save after resizing. The feature lives on S20-era models and newer; if your Gallery lacks the menu, use Method 2.

---

## Method 2: CapCut (Any Android, Free)

CapCut doubles as a perfectly good compressor:

1. Import the video, make no edits (or trim, the biggest size win of all).
2. Tap **Export** and lower the **resolution** (720p for chat sharing) and the **bitrate** slider.
3. Export and share.

The watermark everyone fears comes from a default ending clip that free users can simply select and delete on the timeline before exporting. Some templates force branding, but a plain import-and-export doesn't.

---

## Method 3: A Dedicated Compressor App

The Play Store's compressor apps do one-tap resizing with batch support. The trade-off is uniform: the popular free ones are ad-supported with paid upgrades. Two with a long track record:

- **Panda Video Compressor** - straightforward one-tap presets, 10M+ installs.
- **YouCut** - trimmer and compressor in one, exports without a watermark, 100M+ installs.

Expect ads between actions, and check the output before deleting your original.

<Info>
Google Photos can't export a single video at reduced quality on demand. Its "Storage saver" setting compresses the **cloud backup** (video above 1080p is resized to 1080p), which affects your backups, not a file you're trying to share right now.
</Info>

## Method 4: Don't Compress, Send the Original

If the only reason you're compressing is that the video is "too big to send," there's a way that skips both the quality loss and the wait for a phone processor to re-encode ten minutes of 4K:

1. Open [Transfer.zip](https://transfer.zip) in Chrome on the phone and pick **Quick Transfer**.
2. Select the video from your gallery, any size.
3. Send the link, or have the other device type the 6-digit code.

The original streams directly to the recipient, end-to-end encrypted, full quality. Keep the browser tab in the foreground until it finishes; Android likes to pause background tabs.

## FAQ

**How do I make a video smaller on Android without an app?**
On Samsung, the Gallery editor's "Size and format" option resizes natively. Other Androids don't ship a system compressor, so it's CapCut, a compressor app, or sending a link instead.

**What resolution should I compress to?**
720p for anything watched on a phone, 1080p if it might end up on a TV or monitor. Below 540p, text and detail start smearing.

**Why is my compressed video still huge?**
Length. Resolution changes help, but a 20-minute clip stays big at any watchable quality. Trim to the part that matters, or send the full clip as a link.

**Does WhatsApp compress videos anyway?**
Sent as media, yes, heavily. So compressing *before* WhatsApp just degrades it twice. Either send it as a document in WhatsApp (up to 2 GB) or use a transfer link, and let one tool handle size. Details: [send large files on WhatsApp](/how-to/send-large-files/over-whatsapp).
